# Batch 6b review (second-tier tables brief §11)

## Part A: inn signs of their own

Every core-depth culture (the core cultures and the 6a cultures) draws its inn signs from lists in its own habits, rendered in English (src/data/inn-signs.json, generated by scripts/make-inn-signs.mjs from scripts/inn-signs-data.mjs): at least 12 creatures, 12 objects, 8 trades and 6 colours or metals, and also the persons, descriptions and places a sign is named after. The shared lists stay for the legacy mixes and for the cultures not yet at core depth. A setting's boosted shared words (a port's anchors and nets) still join a culture's own signs where the setting asks for them. The limit is `innLimit` 5 in lens.json `distinctness`: over seeds 1 to 5 no two core-depth cultures share more than 5% of their inn names at the same seed, every pair, in tests/distinctness.test.ts.

Before: 581 inn names shared at the same seed across the 153 pairs of the 18 cultures; 66 pairs over 5%; the largest pair Portuguese/Byzantine at 36.7%. After: 30 shared; none over 5%; the largest Welsh/Irish at 3.3% ("the Old Inn", "the Harp Rest House" before the Irish harp became a brooch). The Welsh and Cornish legacy weight of 0.02, set on 18 November for inns, is restored to 0.1: every limit holds without it.

## Part B: notes

### Mapping changes (B4) and the stored states they alter

`people.json` `resolve` and the lens now read: Ottoman reads Turkic (the `extra: turkish` is dropped, the Turkic table covers it); Swahili and Omani, Phoenician and the modern Malay and maritime Southeast Asian people read Arabic; modern Egyptian reads Arabic (before: the Arabic and Persian table). Every stored state of these five keys, and of the Arabic-and-Persian, steppe, Egyptian, Ethiopian, Bantu and West African keys, now gives different people, fills and group names (their tables are new or written fresh); the tier-6b baseline lists the checked changes and no culture outside the batch changed. The tribal traditions `arabian` and `westAfrican` read the content of Arabic and Persian and of West African; six new tribal traditions (Turkic, Mongol, Arabic, Persian, Egyptian, Ethiopian) start from the steppe and arabian ones.

### Exemptions and judgement calls

- **Mongol, seas:** Mongol has the sea modules' content (lakes, shores and shoals of the steppe's own waters) and meets their targets, so the brief's sea exemption is not needed and none is marked.
- **Deserts:** every 6b culture has desert content at core depth (the brief names Arabic, Persian, Egyptian, Turkic and Mongol; Ethiopian, Bantu and West African have the Danakil, the Kalahari and Namib, and the Sahel's edge, so they were written too).
- **Settlement names:** the cells of the settlement names module are not held to the kind target for any culture (the world engine's template lists are delegated), as before.
- **Stars and space, hyperspace charts, spacecraft, river names:** marked `deferred: styles` for the batch's cultures.
- **Own-entry share:** the eight cultures take 60% or more of own-shape draws from their own entries; Turkic, Mongol, Arabic and Persian give their legacy table 2% and Egyptian, Ethiopian, Bantu and West African 3% (`shares.legacy`).
- **Frame styles:** siblings name in different frames (Turkic by adjective, Mongol by compound, Arabic generic first, Persian by owner, Egyptian by compound; Ethiopian generic first, Bantu by owner, West African by adjective), and each culture of a sibling group turns its 5c and 5d lists by a step of its own and draws its era words from its own place in the shared pools, so two siblings never draw the same word at the same place.
- **Ethiopian lore:** the buda, whose belief has marked craft families, is not used; the zar and the lion of the highlands are.
