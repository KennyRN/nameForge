# Stellar place-name lexicon – review

A for-and-against pass over the other agent's assessment, part by part. For each claim: what holds up, what doesn't, and a revised verdict ready to become nameForge data.

**Scale key** (used in the verdict columns): **Pl** planetary system (a planet and its moons) · **Sy** star system · **Nb** local stellar neighbourhood · **Se** sector · **Ga** galaxy-spanning.

**Verdict key:**
- **Core**: everyday folk generic – high weight, fuses and compounds freely.
- **Formal**: the chart or official form – renders with a folk short form (*Roche Lobe* / *the Lobe*).
- **Landmark**: a named feature or hazard – low weight, rarely settled.
- **Civic**: built places – settlements, stations, infrastructure.
- **Route**: belongs in route names rather than place names.
- **Cut**: dropped from active use.

---

## 1. The framing claims

### 1.1 "Long-lived place words are short, visual and directional"

**For:** English toponymy backs it. The generics that survived a thousand years are monosyllables – *ford, ham, ton, wick, ley, combe*. Real astronomy proves the same drift: the Local Interstellar Cloud is informally the *Local Fluff*; a dark nebula in Crux is simply the *Coalsack*.

**Against:**
- Shortness is a symptom, not the cause. Names survive through use by a community, and long institutional names survive fine when an institution keeps saying them (*Strait of Gibraltar*, *Mount Everest*).
- The shortening examples are too tidy. Real spacefarers already say *L5*, not *Five* – the L5 Society was founded in 1975. Abbreviation and acronym (*Ell-Five*, *H-Two*, *the CMZ*) is at least as likely as the last-element clip (*the Pause*).

**Revised:** keep the principle, but model *three* local-shortening routes, not one: **last element** (*heliopause → the Pause*), **first element** (*Roche Lobe → Roche*), **letter-and-number** (*Lagrange Five → Ell-Five*).

### 1.2 "Lethal features aren't habitable terrain"

**For:** physically right. A photosphere, corona or accretion disc is plasma; no one lives *in* it.

**Against:**
- A name generator isn't a habitability filter. Mariners named the places that killed them – *Cape of Storms*, *Skeleton Coast*, *Devil's Hole*. Hazards get named *because* they're dangerous.
- Names fossilise. English is full of *wood* and *forest* names on land cleared centuries ago; a station called *Corona* can outlive anyone's memory of why.
- Some "events" are permanent classes: most nearby red dwarfs are **flare stars**. *The Flare* is a perfectly natural local nickname for one.

**Revised:** keep lethal features, but as **Landmark** (a feature you name, chart and avoid) and as the *specific* in civic names (*Corona Station*, *Flaretown*), not as generics you live on.

## 3. What the review misses

1. **System-scale features.** For the two smallest scales you asked for, the list is thin. Missing: the **frost line** (*the Frostline*), the **habitable zone**, the **Kuiper belt**, the **scattered disc**, the **Oort cloud**, **Hildas** (Jupiter's 3:2 resonance), **tidal-lock** geography (*terminator*, *dayside*, *nightside*, *twilight band*), *inner* / *outer system*.
2. **Neighbourhood features.** *Local Bubble*, *Local Fluff*, *G-cloud*, *Gould Belt*, *Radcliffe Wave*, OB **associations** and moving **groups**.
3. **Direction words.** Sector and galaxy maps live on them: *coreward, rimward, spinward, trailing*; in a system *sunward, outward, upwell, downwell, above / below the plane*; on a rotating station *spinward, antispinward, hubward, rimward*. (Avoid the exact phrase *Spinward Marches* – Traveller.)
4. **How stars actually get named.** Real conventions, in rough order of distance: proper names (*Sirius*); possessives (*Barnard's Star*, *Kapteyn's Star*); Bayer letters with a constellation genitive (*Tau Ceti*, *Epsilon Eridani*); catalogue numbers (*Gliese 581*, *Ross 128*, *HD 189733*); survey numbers (*Kepler-22*, *TRAPPIST-1*); planet letters (*Kepler-22 b*). nameForge's `star` list only has the first kind – 32 real bright stars – and contains a bug: **Eridani** on its own is a genitive, not a star.
5. **Resemblance naming** for nebulae and clouds (§2.8).
6. **Franchise collisions.** *Outer Rim*, *Mid Rim*, *Core Worlds*, *Kessel Run*, *Wild Space*, *Unknown Regions*, *Spinward Marches*, *Delta Quadrant*, *Neutral Zone*, *Terminus*, *The Expanse*, *Mass Relay*. Single generics stay; exact phrases get blocked.
7. **"Planetary system" is ambiguous.** Astronomers use it for a star's planets. Your scale means a planet and its moons; the guide text should say so.

## 4. "Your duplicates are a feature"

**For:** real English reuses *Head*, *End*, *Hill* at every scale; locals never disambiguate.

**Against:** a *generator* has no locals. Without scale tags it will put *Galactic Rim* and *Rim Crossing* (a ring-gap crossing) in the same batch, and the user can't tell which scale they're reading.

**Revised:** keep duplicates, tag each **sense** with its scales. *Rim* appears once per sense (habitat rim, ring rim, galactic rim) and the scale link chooses which is live.

## 5. The strengthened working list

**For:** the five-way grouping (natural, orbital, flow, formal-to-folk, civic) is a good shape, and every word in it is short and compoundable.

**Against:**
- *Pause, Shock, Sheath* are stellar-wind boundaries filed as gravitational.
- *Horizon* is filed as orbital.
- *Hill* sits in "formal" when it's the most folk word on the page.
- No scale dimension, so it can't drive a scale link.
- Routes and civic words are mixed into place terrain.

**Revised list, by scale** (folk forms; formal forms behind them in §2):

| Scale | Terrain and boundaries | Specific to the scale |

## 6. Their example names

**For:** they follow English compounding (specific + generic, fused or spaced), which nameForge's names engine already does – most could be *generated* rather than listed.

**Against, by group:**
- **Boundary and wind:** strong. *Shockmarch* is excellent (a march is a borderland). *Tailward* is a direction, not a place.
- **Gravity and binaries:** strong. *Barymouth* is a near-homophone of Barmouth in Wales – a happy accident, and proof the fusion rules will produce British-sounding results.
- **Cloud and nebular:** *Knotfall* means nothing (a knot doesn't fall). *Riftwatch* and *Dark Rift* are good; the real *Great Rift* across Cygnus backs them.
- **Galactic:** *Sheet End* reads as comic – light tone only. *Filament Ford* deliberately mixes registers – strange tone.
- **Navigation and civic:** fine, but *Haven Junction* stacks two civic generics; one generic per name is the British norm.

**Their reserve list** (*Flaretown, Photosphere Heights, Accretion Ring, Transit Junction, Bulge Gate*): **for** – right that they're unusual; **against** – nameForge has tones for exactly this. *Flaretown* is a good grim mining camp, *Bulge Gate* a good light one. Weight them down; don't exclude them.
